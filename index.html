Capacity and data

Demand and supply: where a cell really runs out of capacity

Two heatmaps read together. On the demand side, RRC users, timing advance and user throughput show coverage pulling in as load rises, because more throughput needs more PRBs and that lifts the noise floor. On the supply side, PDCCH blocking climbs with user count while PRBs still have headroom, so the scheduler sets the ceiling before the spectrum does.

Capacity and data

Where user throughput breaks, by carrier width

Three months of ROP-level data from two cells on the same band. The 5 MHz carrier drops below 1 Mbps at roughly 16 to 34 RRC users; the 15 MHz carrier holds 4 Mbps until about 48 to 66. Each bin is a sample count, so the break point comes straight from measured data rather than a model.

Capacity and data

Keeping only the precision GPS can resolve

5G geolocation was stored to about 0.1 m against a GPS accuracy of 5 to 10 m. Re-aggregating on a degree-based grid in AWS Redshift took storage from 180 GB a day to about 2 GB, and the analysis window from three weeks to two years, around 500M samples, small enough to run on a laptop.

AI

Three local LLMs, one 3GPP question with a checkable answer

Supported max data rate under TS 38.306 for 4x4 MIMO, MCS 28, 19 RBs. One of three models returned 60.94 Mbps, matching my own implementation. The others did the arithmetic fine but recalled the wrong code rate for that MCS. A first check rather than a controlled comparison.
